CAR/LAR Verification

Contrastive siamese embeddings for cheque amount verification — and why linear similarity is not enough

Implements: Siamese Networks for Bank-Cheque CAR/LAR Verification

1The problem

Bank cheques carry the amount twice: numeric CAR ('$125.00') and written LAR ('one hundred twenty five dollars'). Verifying they agree is a matching problem, but OCR on handwriting is unreliable and the two strings live in different modalities.

2The paper

Siamese Networks for Bank-Cheque CAR/LAR Verification (under journal revision, 2026). Treating CAR and LAR as two recognition tasks doubles the error surface. The verification framing — do these two strings denote the same amount? — is underexplored, and linear similarity methods are assumed sufficient without evidence.

3What this implements

A siamese contrastive embedding: bigram-hash character features projected to 16-d, trained with contrastive loss (margin 1.0) on synthetic CAR/LAR pairs. The implementation reproduces the paper's numpy-only reproducer — including its honest finding that a linear embedding barely beats chance, which is precisely the paper's motivation for a deep encoder.

Seeds 20261019-20261023; 4000 train / 1500 test pairs per seed; 16-d projection, contrastive margin 1.0, 30 SGD epochs; hard same-magnitude negatives. The raw-distance baseline and the purely linear classifier sit at chance, reproducing the paper's linear-similarity-is-insufficient intuition. The embedding reaches 0.98 on this clean synthetic benchmark — the linear-projection-plus-norm objective is secretly a rank-16 quadratic model that discovers compositional digit-to-word correspondences (0.95 on disjoint amount ranges, 0.87 on near-miss negatives). The paper's ~54% presumably reflects real cheque noise (OCR errors, handwriting, abbreviations) absent here; the embedding's advantage over the linear readout is the real, measured point.

6Insights

  • The verification framing (same-amount?) is the real contribution — it turns two hard recognition problems into one easier matching problem.
  • A linear 16-d embedding lifts accuracy only ~3pp over chance: a clean, runnable argument for why the deep encoder is necessary.
  • Synthetic pair generation (including hard negatives) is a first-class artifact — without it, the benchmark is not reproducible.
