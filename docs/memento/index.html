Retention vs Plasticity

The honest trade-off behind 'no catastrophic forgetting': a growing memory that cannot drift-adapt

Implements: Multi-Agent Memento (continual learning without catastrophic forgetting)

1The problem

Continual-learning systems promise to never forget — but a memory that never deletes cannot adapt when the world drifts. The field reports retention wins and buries the plasticity cost.

2The paper

Multi-Agent Memento (continual learning without catastrophic forgetting) (under journal revision, 2026). There is no minimal, runnable demonstration of the retention/plasticity trade-off that lets a practitioner feel both sides of it on the same stream.

3What this implements

One drifting binary stream (shifts at t=700, 1400), two learners: a sliding-window logistic refit (plastic, forgetful) and a never-delete prototype bank (retentive, rigid). Per-segment accuracy shows each winning where the other loses. The implementation is the paper's honest-reality-check design.

The prototype bank retains the early segments but its frozen compromise lags after the shifts; the sliding-window refit tracks the current distribution and dominates the latest segment while forgetting the earlier ones. Neither dominates everywhere: a retention/plasticity trade-off, not a clean win — the paper's honest finding, reproduced.

6Insights

  • 'Eliminates forgetting' is the wrong claim — the right claim is 'trades plasticity for retention', and the exchange rate depends on drift speed.
  • On a stationary stream the memory bank wins; on a drifting stream the refit wins. Know your stream before choosing.
  • The most informative result is where the method loses: the fraud-style drifting segment is the stress test that matters.
