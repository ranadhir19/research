Preliminary investigation: forecasting cardiovascular risk from the resting ECG

Horizon durability, multi-modal redundancy, and a knowledge-graph-guided test of what the measurements mean
Dr Ranadhir Ghosh

Research objective

The question

Cardiovascular disease is the largest cause of death worldwide, and the treatments that prevent it work best when started long before symptoms appear. The resting 12-lead electrocardiogram is the cheapest, most widely recorded cardiac investigation in existence. The question this investigation was set up to answer is whether that recording carries information about cardiovascular risk many years ahead — and specifically whether any such information survives as the prediction window lengthens, or fades.

That distinction is the whole point. A model that scores well at one year and poorly at seven is detecting disease that is already present. A model that holds its value across the window is forecasting. Both look identical if you report a single number, which is what almost all of the published work does.

The gap that motivated this work

A structured harvest of the cardiovascular risk-prediction literature returned 2,096 papers. Two patterns stand out. The first is that long horizons are rarely studied: only about one paper in seven addresses ten years or more, and among ECG studies specifically the count reaching that horizon is in the low tens.

The second is more consequential. The statistical apparatus built precisely for the question of how discrimination behaves over time is almost entirely absent from this literature.

Time-dependent discrimination, censoring weights and landmark designs are the standard tools for the horizon question. A test for a time-varying coefficient — the most direct test of whether an effect decays — appears in 0 of 2,096 papers. Data leakage is discussed in 3. This investigation uses all of them.

Novelty, and the proposed solution

Deep learning on the ECG is not new. Six things here are.

Networks have been trained on electrocardiograms for years, and many report impressive discrimination. The contribution of this work is not another architecture. It is a measurement protocol that makes such claims falsifiable, together with the finding that several standard claims do not survive it — including two made by this investigation itself.

The last row is the general proposal: a synergy screen as a gate on architectural investment. It costs one model fit per subset and would have saved the fusion work reported above.

The synergy screen, stated as a procedure

For candidate view sets A and B, fit the proportional-hazards model on each and on their union, and take the partial-likelihood ratio halved as an additive information measure. Then

II(A;B) = I(A,B) − I(A) − I(B)

If II is positive the views are synergistic and a fusion architecture capable of representing interactions is justified. If II is negative they are redundant, and any such architecture will only add variance — concatenate instead. The quantity is signed, which a difference in concordance is not, and that sign is precisely the decision being made.

This is cheap, it is decided before any architecture is written, and in this study it was correct.

Method and its mathematical basis

Representation learning

A one-dimensional residual network with 1,040,012 parameters is pre-trained on raw 12-lead waveforms by masked-span reconstruction: contiguous segments are hidden and the network must restore them, so it learns waveform structure without using any outcome label. Pre-training uses the training split only; had it seen the test waveforms, every downstream number would be contaminated. Reconstruction loss fell from 0.1926 to 0.1640 before early stopping.

The embedding is then projected by principal components fitted on the training split alone, and the risk model is a proportional-hazards model fitted by exact Newton-Raphson maximisation of the Breslow partial likelihood.

Why the horizon is evaluated the way it is

Three identities drive the whole design.

1. The baseline hazard cancels. For two independent patients under proportional hazards,

   P(T1 < T2 | η1, η2) = 1 / (1 + e−(η1−η2))

The probability of correctly ordering a pair depends only on the difference of linear predictors. The baseline hazard — the only object the horizon acts on — has vanished. Untruncated concordance therefore cannot depend on the horizon at all, and any observed horizon effect must enter through truncation.
2. Censored patients must be retained. Dichotomising at a horizon discarded 69.6% of the test set at five years and left an event-enriched remnant. Uno's censoring-weighted concordance and the cumulative/dynamic time-dependent AUC weight each patient by the reciprocal of the Kaplan-Meier censoring survival Ĝ, so all 28,415 contribute:

   wcase = 1 / Ĝ(Ti)     wcontrol = 1 / Ĝ(τ)
3. Concordance is not a proper score. A concordance index can be improved by a model that ranks well but is badly calibrated. The inverse-probability-weighted Brier score cannot, so it is reported alongside as the index of prediction accuracy:

   IPA(τ) = 1 − BS(τ) / BSKM(τ)
The direct test, which needs no horizon grid at all

Fitting a slope through discrimination measured at a series of horizons is indirect and depends on how the horizons were spaced. There is a test that does not. A proportional-hazards model asserts that a covariate's effect is constant in time; if the ECG contribution genuinely decays, that assertion must fail specifically for the ECG term. The Grambsch-Therneau statistic detects this by testing whether the scaled Schoenfeld residuals for that term trend against time:

χ² = [Σ(gi − ḡ) rik]² / (d · V̄kk · Σ(gi − ḡ)²)

The horizon grid itself was re-run on six-month spacing from one to seven years, the finest the event count supports, replacing the earlier whole-year grid.

The incremental contribution of the ECG, which is what the question is about

The increment stays clearly above zero at every horizon in both designs — the ECG is contributing real information throughout — but it drifts downwards. Whether that drift means anything is the subject of the next section.

Result 3 - testing whether the drift is real

Why this needed a dedicated experiment

Three findings were in tension. The time-variation test says the effect is constant. The incremental concordance drifts down. The incremental proper score drifts up. All three cannot mean what they appear to mean.

The knowledge graph built for this investigation flags exactly this pattern automatically — one edge asserting invariance against the same variable that another edge trends against — and it raised six such tensions. The resolution cannot come from more analysis of the same data. It requires generating data in which the effect is constant by construction, and asking whether the drift appears anyway.

Forty thousand patients per replicate, five replicates, coefficients fixed at the values fitted to the real cohort, censoring times resampled from the 225,306 real censored follow-ups, event rate matched to 3.6 percent. In this synthetic world the ECG effect provably does not decay. Any drift observed is the measuring instrument.

What the simulation settled, in both directions

The rising proper score is an artefact. Under a constant effect the incremental accuracy rises at +0.00515 per year; the observed value is +0.00514. These are indistinguishable. The apparent growth in the ECG's contribution at long horizons is simply what this statistic does when nothing is changing, and it should not be reported as a finding.

The falling concordance is not. Under a constant effect the incremental concordance drifts upward, +0.00044 per year across a null range of [-0.00007, +0.00129]. The observed value, -0.00075, falls outside that range and has the opposite sign. This drift is not the instrument.

This investigation began by hypothesising that the decline was a measurement artefact. That hypothesis was tested directly and is refuted for concordance and confirmed for the proper score. The residual tension — a concordance drift the null cannot explain, alongside a time-variation test that detects nothing — is left standing rather than argued away, and is the most specific open question this work produces.

Four views exist in this cohort, and it matters that three of them are different abstractions of the same physical signal: the expert diagnostic annotations, the learned waveform embedding, and the network's estimate of physiological age are all functions of one ECG. The best single modality is age and sex

Demographics alone reaches a concordance of 0.8091, above every ECG-derived view. The learned embedding reaches 0.7376 and the expert annotations 0.6296. This is not a disappointing result, it is the correct baseline: age is overwhelmingly the dominant predictor of all-cause mortality, and any ECG claim must be measured as a gain over it rather than against chance. A great many published ECG results are reported against chance.

Note the expert annotations' five-year time-dependent AUC of 0.4984. Those labels order patients over the full follow-up, but at a five-year window they are essentially uninformative.

Do the modalities combine?

Every pair is negative. There is no synergy anywhere in this modality set: no two views combine to reveal something neither holds alone. The strongest overlap is between demographics and ECG-estimated age, at 92% redundancy — unsurprising, since that network was trained to predict age.

Architectural sophistication buys nothing here, and that is explainable

Plain concatenation wins. Learned gated attention and score stacking both score lower. This is not a tuning failure — it follows from the interaction information. Sophisticated fusion exists to exploit synergy between modalities, and the measurement above shows there is none to exploit. When views are redundant, the extra parameters of a fusion network only add variance.

The one clear asymmetry is informative: adding the learned embedding to demographics plus expert annotations gains +0.01369 concordance (interval [+0.00939, +0.01811]), whereas adding the annotations to demographics plus the embedding gains only +0.00170 ([+0.00023, +0.00323]). Both intervals exclude zero, but the ratio is roughly eight to one. The learned representation very nearly contains the expert reading; the reverse is far from true.

Which modality would raise the ceiling

Rank by independence, not by reported strength

Rank by independence from the ECG rather than by reported strength, because the measured obstacle here is redundancy, not weakness. On that criterion the first candidate is polygenic risk score: it is the only candidate that is logically incapable of being a downstream consequence of the cardiac state the ECG is measuring, so it is the one modality guaranteed to be non-redundant; it is also cheap and needs collecting only once

Second is coronary artery calcium, because it images the disease substrate directly; the ECG can only register substrate that has already altered conduction. Third is cardiac magnetic resonance, because it is the only candidate that would *test* rather than assume the mechanism attributed to the learned representation, by asking whether the embedding tracks measured fibrosis

The knowledge graph, and what it was for

A graph that does work

A knowledge graph is worth building only if something downstream consumes it. Here it encodes domain and mathematical facts as typed, directed claims, each tagged by where it came from — theorem, measured, or assumption — and converts them into executable constraints on the space of candidate explanations. The tagging is what gives it force: a conclusion resting on an assumption is visibly weaker than one resting on a theorem, and the graph makes that visible rather than leaving it to prose.

Result 5 - how much representation to use depends on the labels

The optimum moves. With 319 labels a four-component projection is best and the full 64 dimensions cost 0.09 AUROC; with all 31,913 labels the ordering reverses and 64 dimensions is best. There is no single correct dimension, only a label-budget-dependent one — a point that matters for any claim about label efficiency, since the answer can be manufactured by choosing where to look.

The combined system: agents, mathematics, and how they compose

Two systems, one ledger

The work is carried by two systems that share a provenance record and a knowledge graph. A survival-modelling pipeline turns signals into calibrated risk and measures it under conditions that can falsify it. A symbolic discovery agent searches for closed-form structure in what that pipeline produces — it is not decoration, it exists because the pipeline has already generated one question it cannot answer numerically.

Two systems are composed. A survival-modelling pipeline turns signals into calibrated risk and measures it honestly; a symbolic discovery agent searches for closed-form structure in what that pipeline produces. They share a knowledge graph and an append-only ledger, so that a claim from either can be traced to the run that produced it and the assumptions it rests on.

Why gates rather than checks

A check that does not stop the run is a comment

The distinction is not stylistic. In an earlier pipeline the split integrity warning was logged and execution continued, and the resulting invalid temporal split reached a written conclusion before anyone noticed. Under the present design that layer refuses: the orchestrator marks the dependent agents as blocked and no verdict is produced at all.

The leakage probe is the same idea inverted. Rather than asserting the absence of leakage, a leak is deliberately planted — follow-up duration is added as a covariate — and the pipeline must detect it. It moved discrimination from 0.800 to 1.000. A pipeline that cannot find a planted leak has no standing to claim there is no unplanted one.

The mathematical discovery agent

What it is for

Search the space of closed-form expressions for structure that satisfies declared constraints, with a critic that can reject a candidate scoring perfectly on residual for being meaningless.

The concrete motivation from this study is Result 3. The constant-effect null established that the incremental concordance drifts in a direction the null cannot produce, while the direct time-variation test detects nothing. That is a question about the functional form of truncated concordance as a function of the event rate, and it is exactly the kind of question a symbolic search can attack and a numerical study cannot.

The representation layer, and why it is the important one

Searching over framings rather than over tokens

Most symbolic regression mutates an expression tree: swap an operator, perturb a constant. That moves a short distance in expression space per step, so reaching a genuinely different framing requires a long and improbable chain of individually unrewarded moves.

The genome here is layered instead. Its levels are representation, symmetry, geometry, basis, scaling, combiner. Mutating the symmetry from none to scale invariant, or the scaling to a similarity variable, relocates the search to a different region in a single step, which a token-level mutation on a tree cannot do. This is why the self-similar heat problem is tractable at all: the similarity variable is a genome setting rather than something that must be assembled operator by operator.

Credit is assigned for results, not for plausibility

Softmax over measured credit, reallocating the next batch across proposers in proportion to success. The definition of success is strict: a proposal is credited only if it ENTERED THE PARETO FRONT, not if it looked plausible.

Self-tuning; a proposer that stops contributing loses budget without being switched off, so it can recover if the landscape changes. In a recorded run the allocation moved [random 0.36, evolution 0.24, critic 0.41] to [random 0.36, evolution 0.28, critic 0.36] during a run, with the critic-guided proposer initially dominant and evolution recovering budget as an elite formed for it to refine. Neither was chosen; both were measured.

The language-model proposer fails closed. If its gateway is unreachable it contributes nothing and the arbiter reallocates. It never silently substitutes a fallback, because a proposer that quietly becomes a different proposer makes the credit assignment meaningless.

The critic, which is what makes the search honest

A perfect score can still be worthless

On the self-similar heat problem the search found the constant solution. Its residual is exactly zero — it satisfies the equation perfectly — and it says nothing whatsoever about heat. A pure residual-minimising search accepts it and reports success.

The critic rejects it as TRIVIAL, and the critic-guided proposer then repairs that specific objection by forcing dependence on the independent variables. The seven rejection classes are:

  • TRIVIAL - identically zero
  • DEGENERATE - no dependence on any independent variable
  • SINGULAR - denominator vanishes inside the domain
  • NOT REAL - complex valued
  • NON-FINITE - fails the finiteness probe
  • SIMPLIFICATION GAP - numerically zero residual the symbolic engine did not reduce, usually an exact solution missed
  • CONSTRAINT - a declared constraint is violated

How the multi-modal system works

The ordering is the point

Conventional practice concatenates everything, trains a fusion network, and discovers afterwards whether it beat the simple baseline. This ordering inverts that: the screen runs first and costs one model fit per subset, and its sign determines whether an interaction-capable architecture is justified at all.

In this study the screen returned negative interaction information for all six pairs. That predicted, before any fusion network was written, that gated attention would not beat concatenation. It did not — it lost by 0.0096 concordance. The screen was right and would have saved the work.

Modalities arrive at different completeness. A modality present in a subset of patients is fitted on that subset and its incremental value reported there, rather than imputed across patients who never had the measurement. Imputing an unmeasured coronary calcium score is inventing a scan.

The electrocardiogram, in detail

The ECG gets the depth because it is the modality the programme is built around, the one where the field is most crowded, and the one where a cardiologist will look hardest. Ten families, 48 features, of which 3 families are currently extractable from the data held.

A twelve-lead ECG has eight independent channels, not twelve

This is the first thing to establish, because it bounds everything after it. Einthoven's law and the definitions of the augmented leads make four of the twelve exact linear combinations of the other two limb leads:

III = II − I     aVR = −(I + II)/2     aVL = I − II/2     aVF = II − I/2

The measurement matrix has rank eight. A model treating the twelve leads as twelve independent sources is fitting four exactly redundant dimensions, which inflates parameter count without adding information — the same estimation problem that Result 5 demonstrates empirically.

It also supplies a free quality check. The reconstruction residual of the four derived leads should be numerically zero. When it is not, a lead is reversed or disconnected, and that recording must be excluded before it becomes spurious signal.

Four ways these features are commonly mishandled

Coronary calcium has a point mass at exactly zero and a long right tail. It is not one distribution and a single log transform mixes two different questions; a two-part model is required.

Cardiac biomarkers are left-censored at the assay detection limit in a substantial fraction of patients. Substituting the limit, or half of it, biases the association, and this substitution is widespread.

Medication is a collider: statin use is caused by high cholesterol and also reduces risk. Conditioning on it without the indication distorts the lipid association rather than adjusting for it.

Body mass index and heart rate are both non-monotone against mortality. A linear term does not merely lose power, it can invert the sign of the reported association.

None of these is exotic. Each is the kind of defect that survives peer review when the feature specification is implicit.

What the ECG buys, in terms a clinician can act on

A concordance moving from 0.8166 to 0.8305 is an abstract quantity. The same change expressed through the risk bands is not.

Using clinical variables alone, the highest-risk fifth carries 62 times the seven-year mortality of the lowest fifth. Adding the ECG representation widens that to 83 times — the lowest-risk fifth falls from 0.31% to 0.24%.

Note where the gain sits. The highest band barely moves; almost all of it comes from more confidently identifying who is not at risk. That is a different clinical proposition from finding more high-risk patients, and it is invisible in any summary statistic. Whether it is the more useful proposition depends on what the prediction would be used for, which is a question for a clinician rather than for this analysis.

What it will be asked, and how the answer gets used

Question. How does truncated concordance depend on the event rate under proportional hazards?

Why it is open. The constant-effect null produces an upward drift in incremental concordance while the observed drift is downward, and no numerical study run here explains the gap.

Constraints it must satisfy, taken directly from the knowledge graph rather than chosen for convenience:

  • C is bounded in [0,1]
  • untruncated concordance is invariant to tau under proportional hazards
  • the cumulative event probability F(tau) is non-decreasing
  • C must depend on tau only through F(tau)
Integration

The resulting expression enters the knowledge graph tagged as a theorem, becomes an executable constraint, and the residual between it and the observed curve becomes the quantity to explain rather than the raw slope

The consequence is concrete. At present the reported quantity is a raw slope, which mixes whatever the metric does with whatever the model does. With a closed form for the first, the reported quantity becomes the residual against it, and that residual is the part that requires a biological explanation. This is the single highest-value piece of unfinished work in the programme.

Until it is done, the open question stands as stated and is not argued away.

Data, not modelling, is what limits these results

Read these as preliminary measurements of a ceiling, not as final estimates

Seven substantive questions remain open at the end of this work. Every one of them is blocked by a property of the available data rather than by modelling effort. Not one would be resolved by a larger network, longer training, better hyperparameters or a more elaborate fusion scheme. That is the single most important conclusion of this investigation, and it is what makes the results preliminary in a precise rather than a rhetorical sense.

The numbers reported above are therefore best read as a calibration of where the ceiling currently sits, and of how much data would move it.

What becomes possible, stated conditionally

These are conditional statements, not predictions. Each holds if the incoming cohort carries the stated property.

If it carries ten or more years of follow-up, the governing question becomes answerable for the first time, and at any scale above roughly seven thousand patients it becomes answerable with a usefully narrow interval rather than a borderline one.

If it carries adjudicated cardiovascular endpoints, the largest gap in this work closes. Everything reported here is against all-cause death, and a model that ranks death well may rank cardiovascular death poorly; those two questions separate exactly where the clinical target lies.

If it carries examination dates, a genuine temporal split becomes possible and the deployment question — does this hold for patients seen after the model was fitted — can be asked at all. It currently cannot.

If it carries laboratory values, imaging or inherited risk, the redundancy ceiling measured in Result 4 can finally be tested rather than merely reported. Every view available here is a function of one ECG, so the finding that they are mutually redundant is almost a foregone conclusion. Whether a genuinely independent modality is synergistic is an open and much more interesting question, and the screen to answer it is already built.

If it comes from a different health system, transportability moves from an internal covariate-shift proxy to a real external validation — something only 22 percent of the screened literature achieves.

What would not change

More data does not repair the methodology, and it is worth being explicit that the corrections in this report would still be required at any scale. A larger cohort measured with a single-horizon AUC, compared against chance rather than against age, with censored patients discarded and no null calibration, would produce more confident wrong answers rather than fewer. The protocol has to travel with the data.

Scale also does not make the redundancy result go away. If the additional views are further functions of the same ECG, a million patients will establish the same ceiling more precisely.

Conclusions

What this preliminary investigation establishes

A learned ECG representation carries real incremental prognostic information. It adds roughly 0.010 to 0.020 concordance over clinical variables, with intervals excluding zero under every design tried, and it very nearly subsumes expert diagnostic annotation of the same waveform.

The effect shows no detectable change over time by the most direct test available (p = 0.94), and the incremental contribution remains clearly positive at every horizon from one to seven years.

Two apparent horizon trends were tested against a constant-effect null. The rising proper score is entirely an artefact of that statistic and should not be reported. The falling concordance is not an artefact and remains unexplained.

The available modalities are mutually redundant. No pair shows synergy, and sophisticated fusion loses to concatenation. Raising the ceiling requires a modality that is not derived from the ECG, and the strongest argument is for one that cannot be — inherited risk.

Every remaining question is blocked by data, not by modelling. All seven open questions require a property the present cohort lacks: longer follow-up, adjudicated endpoints, examination dates, an independent institution, or a modality that is not a function of the ECG. None would yield to a larger network.

What this suggests is possible

The value of a preliminary study is in what it makes tractable next, and on that reading the outlook is favourable rather than discouraging.

The signal is real and measurable at a scale that is already available. The instrument for detecting horizon decay now exists, has been calibrated against a null, and has already overturned four conclusions — two of them this investigation's own. The data requirement to settle the governing question turns out to be thousands of patients rather than hundreds of thousands, which places it well inside the reach of a single large clinical collaboration.

What has not yet been tested, and is the most promising thing available, is whether a modality genuinely independent of the ECG behaves differently. Every view examined here is a function of one ten-second recording, so their mutual redundancy was close to inevitable. The screen that would settle whether inherited risk, laboratory chemistry or cardiac imaging is synergistic with the waveform is built, validated and costs one model fit per subset. It is waiting on data, not on method.
